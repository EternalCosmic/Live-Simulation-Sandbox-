Slide 3: Definition of Space

What is "Space"?

  • Space is closer than you think (only a 1-hour drive straight up at highway speed!).
  • Getting up there is easy; staying there is the hard part.
  • The Kármán Line (100 km): The international boundary line where atmospheric lift becomes negligible.
  • The Paradox: Space isn't difficult because it's high up; it's difficult because it's empty.
Slide 4: Part 1 - Propulsion

Leaving Earth: Newton's Third Law

  • Action & Reaction: For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.
  • Momentum Conservation: Expelling high-velocity gas backward propels the vehicle forward.
  • Rockets work in the vacuum of space because they do not rely on pushing against air.
  • The Challenge: Overcoming atmospheric resistance and Earth's heavy gravitational pull.
Slide 5: Rocket Dynamics

The Tyranny of the Rocket Equation

  • Tsiolkovsky’s Rocket Equation: To accelerate propellants, you need additional propellants to carry that weight.
  • Mass Breakdown: Typical orbital vehicles are ~90% propellant, ~9% rocket structure, and only ~1% usable payload.
  • Staging: Dropping heavy, empty metal tanks mid-flight to shed dead weight.
  • Max Q: The region of maximum dynamic aerodynamic stress during flight.
Slide 6: Orbital Mechanics

What is an Orbit? (Falling Constantly)

  • An orbit does not mean escaping gravity! (The ISS experiences ~90% of Earth's surface gravity).
  • An orbit is simply falling toward Earth and continuously missing it.
  • You miss the planet by accelerating horizontally faster than gravity pulls you down.
Slide 7: Orbital Speeds

The Speed of Space

  • Low Earth Orbit (LEO) Velocity: ~17,500 mph (28,000 km/h) or 7.8 km/s.
  • At this speed, a spacecraft orbits the entire Earth once every 90 minutes.
  • Too slow: Re-enters the atmosphere and crashes.
  • Too fast: Exceeds escape velocity and breaks orbit into deep space.
